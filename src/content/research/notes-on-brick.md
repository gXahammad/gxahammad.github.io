Brick is everywhere in Bangladesh. It is made from the same river soil the country stands on, fired in kilns at the edge of every town, and laid by builders who learned the craft from their parents.

For architects it is a generous material: it can carry a building, shade it, let it breathe through a screen of open joints, and age well in the rain.

It also has a cost. Traditional kilns are a major source of air pollution, and the best clay is the same soil that feeds the country. Building with brick today means building with less of it — thinner walls where possible, screens instead of solid walls, and new kilns that burn cleaner.
